Unit 1 — Introduction to Company Law
Meaning & Definition of a Company
Sec 2(20)
▼
Lord Macnaghten (Salomon v. Salomon, 1897): "A company is an abstract person; it is a legal person created by law, clothed in law and carrying on activities through law."
Sec 2(20) Companies Act 2013: "Company means a company incorporated under this Act or under any previous company law."

A company is a voluntary association of persons formed for a lawful purpose. It has a legal personality separate from its members and is capable of owning property, entering contracts, and suing/being sued in its own name.

Characteristics of a Company
  • Separate Legal Entity — distinct from members (Salomon v. Salomon)
  • Limited Liability — members' liability limited to unpaid share capital
  • Perpetual Succession — members may change; company continues
  • Common Seal — official signature of the company (now optional post-2015)
  • Transferability of Shares — freely transferable in public companies
  • Separate Property — company owns its own assets (Macaura v. Northern Assurance)
  • Capacity to Sue and Be Sued
  • Artificial Legal Person — not a natural person; cannot be a citizen of India (State Trading Corp. case)
A company is NOT a citizen (State Trading Corp. v. CTO 1963), so it cannot claim fundamental rights under Art. 19. But it CAN claim rights under Art. 14 (equality).
Salomon v. Salomon & Co. [1897]
Macaura v. Northern Assurance [1925]
State Trading Corp. v. CTO [1963]
Types of Companies
OPC · Small · Associate · Dormant
▼
One Person Company (OPC) — Sec 2(62) Has only one member who is a natural Indian resident. Must nominate a person who becomes member if original member dies/incapacitated. No minimum paid-up capital. Cannot convert to Sec 8 company. Cannot carry out Non-Banking Financial Investment activities.
Small Company — Sec 2(85) Paid-up capital ≤ ₹4 Cr (not exceeding ₹10 Cr); OR turnover ≤ ₹40 Cr (not exceeding ₹100 Cr). Excludes holding/subsidiary, Sec 8, or companies under special Acts. Benefits: No cash flow statement, abridged annual return, only 2 board meetings/year.
Associate Company — Sec 2(6) Company in which another company has significant influence (at least 20% of total voting power OR control under agreement) but is NOT a subsidiary. Includes Joint Ventures.
Dormant Company — Sec 455 Company formed for future project or to hold asset/intellectual property; no significant accounting transactions for 2 consecutive years. Can apply to ROC for dormant status. Minimum 3 directors; must file annual return. Allowed to remain dormant for max 5 years.
Section 8 Company (Assoc. Not for Profit) Promotes arts, science, commerce, education, religion, charity, social welfare, sports, environment. Profits applied only for objects. Cannot pay dividends. E.g., FICCI, CII.
Illegal Association — Sec 464 Association of >50 persons formed for profit without registering = Illegal Association. Every member personally liable for all debts; liable to fine of ₹1 lakh. Cannot sue others but can be sued.
OPC cannot be converted to Sec 8 Company and cannot carry on NBFI activities. For Illegal Association, >50 persons for profit without registration = illegal.
Lifting of Corporate Veil
Judicial + Statutory
▼

The corporate veil is the legal shield that separates the company from its members. Courts and statutes can "pierce" this veil in certain situations to hold members personally liable.

Judicial Grounds 1. Fraud or Improper Conduct — Gilford Motor Co. v. Horne
2. Tax Evasion — Bacha F. Guzdar v. CIT; Vodafone case
3. Agency — Parent liable for agent-subsidiary acts — Smith Stone & Knight v. Birmingham
4. Enemy Character — war-time determination — Daimler Co. v. Continental Tyre
5. Sham/Façade — used as a device to evade legal obligations
⚖️
Statutory Grounds 1. Reduction below min. members (Sec 3A)
2. Fraudulent trading (Sec 339)
3. Misrepresentation in prospectus (Sec 35)
4. Holding-Subsidiary relationship
5. Failure to return app money (Sec 39)
6. Ultra Vires acts of directors
Salomon principle = Separate Legal Entity. Lifting the veil = an EXCEPTION to Salomon. Always mention which ground applies to the fact scenario.
Promoters & Pre-Incorporation Contracts
Legal Position · Liability
▼
Promoter (Sec 2(69)): A person who has been named as such in a prospectus OR who has control over the affairs of the company, directly or indirectly, whether as a shareholder, director or otherwise.
Legal Position of Promoter Not a trustee, not an agent (company doesn't exist yet). Stands in a fiduciary position — must not make secret profits. Must disclose all material facts. Cases: Erlanger v. New Sombrero Phosphate Co.
Pre-Incorporation Contracts Contracts made BEFORE company is incorporated. Company cannot ratify pre-incorporation contracts — it didn't exist. Promoters remain personally liable UNLESS company enters a fresh contract (novation). Kelner v. Baxter (1866)
KEY: Company CANNOT ratify pre-incorporation contracts. Only a fresh contract (novation/substituted contract) relieves promoters. Remember Kelner v. Baxter.
Kelner v. Baxter [1866]
Erlanger v. New Sombrero Phosphate [1878]

NCLT & NCLAT — Administration of Company Law
Sec 407–434
▼
NCLT — National Company Law Tribunal Constituted under Sec 408, effective 1 June 2016. Quasi-judicial body. Has 11 benches (1 Principal at New Delhi + 10 Regional: Ahmedabad, Allahabad, Bengaluru, Chandigarh, Chennai, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai). Replaced Company Law Board, BIFR, AAIFR.
NCLAT — National Company Law Appellate Tribunal Hears appeals from NCLT orders. Also hears appeals from orders of CCI (Competition Commission of India). Further appeal lies to Supreme Court of India.
NCLT consolidates jurisdiction of: Company Law Board + Board for Industrial & Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) + Appellate Authority for Industrial & Financial Reconstruction (AAIFR) + High Court jurisdiction related to winding up.

Unit 2 — Documents
Memorandum of Association (MOA)
Charter Document
▼

The MOA is the charter/constitution of the company. It defines the company's scope and limits. Sec 2(56): "Memorandum means the memorandum of association of a company as originally framed or as altered from time to time in pursuance of any previous company law or this Act."

Clauses of MOA 1. Name Clause
2. Registered Office/Situation Clause
3. Object Clause (main + ancillary objects)
4. Liability Clause (limited by shares/guarantee)
5. Capital Clause (authorised share capital)
6. Subscription/Association Clause
Alteration of MOA Name Clause: Central Govt approval + SR
Registered Office: within same RoC — Board; same state — SR; different state — CLT + SR
Object Clause: Special Resolution
Liability Clause: members' consent in writing
Capital Clause: Ordinary Resolution
MOA prevails over AOA in case of conflict. AOA prevails over neither the Act nor the MOA.
Articles of Association (AOA)
Internal Regulations
▼

AOA contains the rules and regulations for internal management of the company. Every company except public unlimited company MUST have AOA. Default: Table F (public company limited by shares).

  • AOA is subordinate to both MOA and the Companies Act
  • Can be altered by Special Resolution — alteration must be bona fide for benefit of company as a whole
  • Cannot contain provisions inconsistent with the Companies Act
  • Alteration cannot force members to take more shares or increase liability without written consent
Allen v. Gold Reefs of West Africa [1900]
Greenhalgh v. Arderne Cinemas [1951]
In conflict between MOA and AOA — MOA prevails. In conflict between AOA and Act — Act prevails. Hierarchy: Act > MOA > AOA.
Doctrine of Ultra Vires
Beyond Powers
▼
An act is ultra vires if it is beyond the legal powers/authority of the company as defined in the MOA. Such acts are void, cannot be ratified even by unanimous consent of ALL shareholders.
Effects of Ultra Vires Transaction • Void and unenforceable
• Cannot be ratified
• Directors personally liable
• Injunction can be obtained
• Property: if traceable, can be recovered
Intra Vires vs Ultra Vires Intra vires = within powers (valid)
Ultra vires company = void, even if shareholders unanimously ratify
Ultra vires directors (but intra vires company) = CAN be ratified by shareholders
Ashbury Railway Carriage Co. v. Riche [1875]
Ultra vires the COMPANY = cannot be ratified. Ultra vires the DIRECTORS (but within company powers) = CAN be ratified. This distinction is crucial in exam problems.
Doctrine of Constructive Notice & Indoor Management
Most Important — Unit 2
▼
Constructive Notice MOA & AOA are public documents registered with ROC. Every outsider dealing with company is deemed to have notice of their contents. Cannot claim ignorance. Protects the COMPANY against outsiders.
⚡
Indoor Management (Turquand's Rule) An outsider acting in good faith may ASSUME that internal requirements of the company (as permitted by MOA/AOA) have been duly complied with. Protects OUTSIDERS from the company. Also called "Turquand's Rule."
Royal British Bank v. Turquand [1856]
Exceptions to Indoor Management (where it does NOT protect outsiders)
  • Knowledge of irregularity — outsider knows of internal irregularity
  • Suspicion of irregularity — circumstances arouse suspicion
  • Forgery — document is forged; cannot be ratified
  • Outsider is an insider — director or officer of the company
  • Act is ultra vires the company itself
Constructive Notice: Protects COMPANY. Indoor Management: Protects OUTSIDER. They are exceptions to each other!
Prospectus — Types, Misstatement & Liabilities
Sec 2(70) · Sec 34–38
▼
Sec 2(70): Any document described/issued as prospectus; any notice/circular/advertisement inviting (1) subscriptions for shares/debentures AND (2) made to public at large.
Shelf Prospectus For financial institutions; valid for 1 year from first offer. No need to file prospectus for each subsequent offer within that period.
Red Herring Prospectus Filed before price fixation. Does not have complete particulars of price/quantum. Price filled in later.
Abridged Prospectus Shortened version accompanying application form. Must contain all material info.
Misstatement in Prospectus (Sec 34–38)

Misstatement = untrue statement + suppression of material facts + omission of material facts. "Half a truth is no better than a downright falsehood." — Rex v. Kylsant

Guilty Persons (Sec 35) 1. Directors
2. Proposed Directors (named in prospectus)
3. Promoters
4. Authoriser of prospectus
5. Experts (who consented)
Civil Liability (Sec 35) Aggrieved allottee can claim compensation. Director NOT liable if: (a) withdrew consent before issue, (b) issued without knowledge, (c) gave public notice after becoming aware. Amendment 2017: Relief if relied on expert's misleading statement in good faith.
Criminal Liability (Sec 34): Fine not less than fraud amount, extendable to 3× + imprisonment 6 months to 10 years. Public Offer: Must be made to >200 persons to be "public at large" (now: any identified persons exceeding limit = public offer).
Rex v. Kylsant
Nash v. Lynde (private communication ≠ public)
Companies Amendment Act 2017: Private Placement (Sec 42) — offer to ≤50 identified persons per financial year (excl. QIBs and ESOP employees). Failure to file return of allotment within 15 days → ₹1000/day penalty, max ₹25 lakhs.

Unit 3 — Management: Directors, Meetings & Committees
Classification & Types of Directors
Sec 149–165
▼
Min DirectorsOPC: 1 | Pvt: 2 | Public: 3
Max Directors15 (SR needed to exceed)
Max Directorships20 companies (max 10 public)
Independent Director (Sec 149(4)) ≥1/3 of total directors to be Independent. Must: be a person of integrity, not a promoter/related to promoter, no pecuniary relationship with company in preceding 2 years, not KMP in preceding 3 years. Max tenure: 2 consecutive terms of 5 years each. Cool-off: 3 years before re-appointment.
Additional Director (Sec 161(1)) Appointed by Board per AOA. Holds office till next AGM. Cannot be someone who failed to get elected at GM. Must collect DIN, consent DIR-2, interest disclosure MBP-1.
Alternate Director (Sec 161(2)) Appointed by Board (if authorized by AOA or SR). Represents absent director (absent >3 months from India). Vacates office when original director returns. Cannot act after original director's term ends.
Casual Vacancy (Sec 161(4)) Only Public companies (or private subsidiary of public) can fill by Board resolution. Private companies must fill at General Meeting. Acts till remaining tenure of replaced director. Amendment 2017: all companies including private may fill by Board, to be approved at next GM.
Women Director (Sec 149(1)) Mandatory for: (a) every listed company, (b) public company with paid-up capital ≥₹100 Cr or turnover ≥₹300 Cr. At least one woman director.
Small Shareholders' Director (Sec 151) Listed company may elect one. Small shareholder = nominal value ≤ ₹20,000. Requires notice from ≥1000 small shareholders or 1/10th of total. Tenure: 3 consecutive years. Not eligible for reappointment. Max 2 companies simultaneously.
Amendment 2017: Directorship in a dormant company shall NOT be counted in the 20 company limit.
Director Identification Number (DIN) & Removal
Sec 153 · Sec 169
▼
DIN (Sec 153) Every person to be appointed as director MUST have DIN. Apply in DIR-3 to Central Government. Only an individual can be director. No Body Corporate/Firm can be a director. One person = one DIN for lifetime.
Removal under Sec 169 (by shareholders) 1. Give special notice to company (before 14 days of meeting)
2. Company sends copy to director concerned
3. Director has right to be heard at meeting
4. Pass Ordinary Resolution (simple majority)
Note: Cannot remove directors appointed by Central Govt or by proportional representation.
Vacation of Office (Sec 167)
  • Incurs disqualification under Sec 164
  • Absents from all Board Meetings for 12 months (with or without leave)
  • Fails to disclose interest in contracts
  • Convicted and sentenced to imprisonment ≥6 months (even if appeal filed)
  • Disqualified by order of court/tribunal
Key Managerial Personnel (KMP)
Sec 2(51) · Sec 203
▼
KMP = whole-time employees vested with most important roles/responsibilities; monitor and achieve goals targeted by Board.
  • Managing Director OR CEO OR Manager
  • Company Secretary
  • Whole-time Director
  • Chief Financial Officer
  • Any officer not more than one level below directors in whole-time employment designated as KMP by Board
Applicability (Sec 203) Mandatory for: Every Listed Company + Every other public company with paid-up capital ≥₹10 Cr + Every private company with paid-up capital ≥₹10 Cr (for Company Secretary).
An individual cannot be both Chairman AND MD/CEO simultaneously — unless AOA provides otherwise OR company doesn't carry multiple businesses.
Company Meetings — AGM, EGM & Requisites
Sec 96–122 | Most Important
▼
Annual General Meeting (AGM) — Sec 96 Every company must hold AGM. First AGM: within 9 months of closing of first financial year. Subsequent AGMs: within 6 months of financial year end, and gap between 2 AGMs ≤15 months. During business hours (9am–6pm), on a non-holiday. In city/town of registered office.
Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) — Sec 100 Any GM other than AGM. Called by Board on its own OR on requisition by members (at least 1/10th of paid-up share capital or 1/10th of members in companies without share capital). If Board fails to call within 21 days → members themselves can call within 3 months.
Requisites of a Valid Meeting
  • Proper Authority — Board or requisitionists
  • Proper Notice — 21 clear days written notice; 20% members can accept shorter notice (AGM: 95%)
  • Quorum — Public co.: 5 members personally present (if ≤1000 members), 15 (1000–5000), 30 (>5000). Private co.: 2 members personally present
  • Agenda — Notice must specify business (ordinary and special)
  • Chairman — presides over meeting
  • Minutes — must be maintained within 30 days of conclusion
Ordinary Resolution Votes in favour > votes against (simple majority). For ordinary business at AGM.
Special Resolution Votes in favour ≥ 3× votes against. Required for: altering MOA/AOA, voluntary winding up, change of registered office to another state. File copy with ROC within 30 days.
Postal Ballot (Sec 110): Used for specific matters (e.g., change in object clause, buy-back, foreign investments). E-voting to be provided by listed companies for all matters. Video Conferencing: Directors can attend board meetings via VC — counts towards quorum.
Board Committees — Audit, NRC, SRC, CSR
Sec 177–135
▼
Audit Committee (Sec 177) Mandatory for: every listed public company + public co. paid-up capital ≥₹10 Cr + turnover ≥₹100 Cr + outstanding loans/borrowings/debentures/deposits ≥₹50 Cr. Minimum 3 directors, majority must be independent. Chairperson must be able to read financial statements. Functions: recommend auditor appointment, review financial statements, examine audit report, approve related party transactions, evaluate internal controls.
Nomination & Remuneration Committee (Sec 178) Mandatory for same class as Audit Committee. Min 3 non-executive directors, majority independent. Functions: identify persons qualified to become directors/KMP, recommend to Board, formulate criteria for evaluation of directors, recommend remuneration policy. Policy to be placed on website; salient features in Board's report.
Stakeholders Relationship Committee (Sec 178) Mandatory if company has >1000 shareholders/debenture holders/deposit holders/other security holders. Chaired by non-executive director. Functions: resolve grievances of security holders (transfer, non-receipt of dividend, non-receipt of share certificates, etc.)
CSR Committee (Sec 135) Applicable if: net worth ≥₹500 Cr OR turnover ≥₹1000 Cr OR net profit ≥₹5 Cr during any preceding FY. Min 3 directors, at least 1 independent. Must spend ≥2% of average net profits of preceding 3 years on CSR activities (Schedule VII). Functions: formulate CSR policy, recommend expenditure, monitor CSR activities. Amendment 2020: Unspent amount to be transferred to specified fund within 6 months of FY end.
Amendment 2020 Sec 177/178: Penalty for contravention — Company: ₹5 lakhs; every defaulting officer: ₹1 lakh.
Insider Trading (SEBI Regulations 2015)
Sec 3 Unit 3
▼
Insider trading = trading or dealing in securities based on UPSI (Unpublished Price Sensitive Information). Objective: prohibit insiders from profiting from UPSI; ensure investor confidence in market.
Who is an Insider? Person in possession of/access to UPSI OR a Connected Person. Includes persons with contractual, fiduciary or employment relationship with company; also "deemed connected persons" — immediate relatives, holding/subsidiary companies, intermediaries, investment companies, AMCs, officials of stock exchange, mutual fund trustees.
UPSI (Unpublished Price Sensitive Information) Financial results, dividends, change in capital structure, mergers/acquisitions/demergers, change in KMP, material litigation. Insider must not trade during "trading window closure" period.
Penalties: SEBI can impose fine of ₹25 Crore OR 3× profits made (whichever higher) (Sec 15G). Criminal: imprisonment up to 10 years (Sec 24, SEBI Act). Reputation risk.
Amendment 2022: Regulations now apply to units of Mutual Funds. No insider can communicate/provide access to UPSI to ANY person including other insiders.

Unit 4 — Winding Up, Whistleblowing & CSR
Winding Up — Concept & Modes (Sec 270)
Compulsory + Voluntary
▼
Winding up = the last stage of a company's existence where all assets are realized and used to pay off creditors, shareholders and other liabilities. The company ceases to exist after winding up.
Compulsory Winding Up (by Tribunal) 1. Unable to pay debts
2. Company itself passes SR to wind up by Tribunal
3. Acts against interest/integrity/security of India or spoils relations with foreign countries
4. Not filed financial statements or annual returns for preceding 5 consecutive FYs
5. Just and equitable grounds
6. Fraudulent activities or unlawful business; guilty of fraud/misconduct in formation
VS
Voluntary Winding Up Initiated by members or creditors without Court intervention.

Members' Voluntary: Requires Declaration of Solvency. Company is solvent — can pay all debts within 3 years.

Creditors' Voluntary: When company is insolvent. Creditors' meeting held within 14 days of members' meeting. Creditors appoint liquidator.
Petition for Compulsory Winding Up (Sec 272)
  • Petition filed in Form 1/2/3 in 3 sets
  • Can be presented by: the company, creditors, contributories, Registrar, Central Govt, State Govt, or any person authorized by Central Govt
  • Company Liquidator appointed by Tribunal
Just & Equitable grounds: Loss of substratum, deadlock in management, oppression of minority, fraudulent purpose. Key case for deadlock — Ebrahimi v. Westbourne Galleries [1973].
Whistleblowing — Vigil Mechanism (Sec 177(9))
Sec 177 · Key Topic
▼
Whistleblowing = calling attention of top management to wrongdoing within an organization. Origin: British policemen blowing whistles on observing crimes.
Types of Whistleblowers Internal: Employee, superior officer, or designated officer within the organization
External: Lawyers, media, law enforcement, watchdog agencies — outside the organization
Objectives • Encourage employees to report ethical/legal violations internally
• Minimize exposure to damage from circumventing internal mechanisms
• Show organization is serious about code of conduct
Vigil Mechanism — Companies Act 2013 (Sec 177(9))
  • Mandatory for ALL listed companies
  • Companies accepting deposits from public
  • Companies borrowing from Banks/PFI in excess of ₹50 Crores
  • Must be operated through Audit Committee (or if conflict of interest, others on committee deal)
  • For other companies: Board nominates a director to play role of Audit Committee
  • Adequate safeguard against victimization of directors/employees using the mechanism
  • Direct access to Chairperson of Audit Committee in exceptional cases
  • Details must be disclosed on company website and in Board's Report
  • Repeated frivolous complaints: Audit Committee may take action including reprimand
Penalty: Company: minimum ₹1 lakh, extendable to ₹5 lakhs. Defaulting officer: imprisonment up to 1 year OR fine ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh OR both.
Vigil Mechanism under Listing Agreement (Clause 49) is NON-MANDATORY. Under Companies Act 2013 (Sec 177(9)) — MANDATORY for specified companies.

Unit 5 — Prevention of Oppression & Mismanagement
Oppression & Mismanagement — Meaning (Sec 241)
Sec 241–245
▼
Oppression (Sec 241(1)(a)) Conduct of company's affairs in a manner prejudicial to public interest OR oppressive to any member/members OR prejudicial to the interests of the company. Key case: Elder v. Elder & Watson (1952) — oppression = burdensome, harsh, and wrongful conduct. Must be continuing course of conduct. Single act is generally NOT oppression.
Mismanagement (Sec 241(1)(b)) A material change has taken place OR is likely to take place in the management/control of the company (change in Board, manager, managing/whole-time director; acquisition of shares; change via insolvency/amalgamation) AND such change is/likely to be prejudicial to company or public interest.
Who Can File Application (Sec 244)
  • Company with share capital: not less than 100 members OR 1/10th of total members OR members holding not less than 1/10th of issued share capital — whichever is less
  • Company without share capital: not less than 1/5th of total members
  • Central Government can also refer the matter
Powers of Tribunal (Sec 242) & Class Action (Sec 245)
Relief · Remedies
▼
Powers of Tribunal (Sec 242) • Regulation of conduct of affairs of company
• Purchase of shares/interests by other members or company itself
• Restriction of transfer of shares
• Terminate/set aside/modify any agreement with MD/director/manager
• Removal of MD/Director/Manager
• Recovery of undue gains made by MD/Director/Manager
• Winding up of company (if no other remedy adequate)
Class Action (Sec 245) Filed by required number of members/depositors. Purpose: restrain company from ultra vires acts, breach of MOA/AOA, claim damages against company/directors/auditors for fraudulent/unlawful conduct, seek any remedy Tribunal deems fit.
Tribunal: issues public notice, consolidates similar applications, appoints lead applicant.
Amendment 2019 — Sec 242: Tribunal must record decision on whether respondent is a fit and proper person to hold office of director. If declared unfit: cannot hold directorship for 5 years from Tribunal's order.
Certified copy of Tribunal order must be filed with ROC within 30 days. Tribunal can also make interim orders.

Unit 6 — Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, 2016
Need for IBC, 2016 & Overall Scheme
Background
▼
The IBC, 2016 was enacted to modernize India's outdated bankruptcy laws. Previous laws were cumbersome — took several years to resolve. IBC provides a time-bound, efficient process to maximize asset value and improve recovery rates for creditors.
Key Features of IBC • Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)
• Fast-track CIRP (for SMEs/low complexity cases)
• Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) as regulator
• Insolvency Professionals (IP) to manage affairs during CIRP
• Committee of Creditors (CoC) with voting power
• Priority-based distribution of proceeds
Key Stakeholders • NCLT: Adjudicating Authority
• NCLAT: Appellate Authority
• IBBI: Regulator (registers IPs, enforces IBC)
• IRP/RP: Manages company during CIRP
• CoC: Committee of financial creditors (66% voting for plan approval)
• Financial Creditors vs Operational Creditors
IBC Amendment 2020 (COVID-19): Minimum threshold for filing application raised from ₹1 lakh to ₹1 crore (Proviso to Sec 4 — Central Govt notified on March 24, 2020). Protects businesses from frivolous insolvency filings during economic stress.
Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)
Most Important — Unit 6
▼

CIRP is the main mechanism under IBC for resolution of corporate insolvency. It is time-bound — 180 days (extendable by 90 days, total max 270 days; post-2019 amendment: 330 days including litigation).

Step-by-Step CIRP Process
  • Step 1 — Initiation: Financial creditor, operational creditor, or corporate debtor itself files application before NCLT. Default must be >₹1 crore. NCLT within 14 days admits or rejects.
  • Step 2 — Moratorium: Once admitted, NCLT declares moratorium. No legal proceedings against company/assets. Management handed over to IRP (Insolvency Resolution Professional).
  • Step 3 — Appointment of IRP: NCLT appoints IRP. IRP takes over management, makes public announcement, collects claims from creditors.
  • Step 4 — Verification of Claims: IRP verifies claims and prepares list of creditors (financial + operational).
  • Step 5 — Formation of CoC: Committee of Creditors formed comprising financial creditors. Operational creditors' representative can attend but has no voting right unless aggregate debt >10% of total.
  • Step 6 — Formulation of Resolution Plan: IRP/RP invites resolution plans from interested parties (including promoters). Plan must be submitted within specified time.
  • Step 7 — Approval of Plan: CoC evaluates and approves by at least 66% voting share of financial creditors. Plan then sent to NCLT for approval.
  • Step 8 — Implementation OR Liquidation: If plan approved → implement. If not approved within timeline → company goes into liquidation. Liquidator distributes proceeds per IBC priority.
Moratorium = No suits/proceedings against company; no transfer/disposal of assets; no foreclosure. Duration = from date of admission of application till completion of CIRP.
Committee of Creditors (CoC) & Documentation
Sec 21 IBC
▼
Committee of Creditors (CoC) Comprises financial creditors. Functions: supervise IRP, monitor CIRP progress, protect creditors' interests, vote on key decisions (66% threshold to approve resolution plan), authorize sale of assets, appoint liquidator if plan fails, distribute proceeds per IBC priority.
Key Documents in CIRP • Application for Insolvency Proceedings
• Demand Notice (operational creditors)
• Verification of Claims list
• Insolvency Resolution Plan
• Minutes of CoC/NCLT meetings
• Progress/Status/Final Reports by IRP
• Various Notices (CoC meetings, stakeholder meetings, NCLT hearings)
Priority of Distribution in Liquidation 1. Insolvency resolution costs
2. Secured creditors + workmen dues (24 months)
3. Unsecured creditors
4. Government dues
5. Remaining debts and dues
6. Preference shareholders
7. Equity shareholders
Moratorium (Sec 14) Declared upon admission of application by NCLT. Prohibits: institution of suits/proceedings; transfer/encumbrance of assets; recovery of property by owner/lessor; termination of supply of essential goods/services. Ends upon: completion of CIRP or commencement of liquidation.
Fast-track CIRP: Available for small companies with low complexity; must be completed in shorter period.
